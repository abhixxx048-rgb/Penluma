# 9. Newsletter Business

> **Time to first income:** 2-4 months | **Startup cost:** $0-100 | **Beginner earnings:** $0-300/month | **Experienced earnings:** $1,000-5,000/month | **Difficulty:** 3/5 | **AI-Future-Proof:** 3/5

## What Is It, Really?

A newsletter is an email you send on a regular schedule — weekly, for example — to people who signed up for it. You pick one topic (personal finance, AI tools, local news, fantasy football), write something useful, and grow a list of subscribers — people who asked to receive your emails. Once enough of the right people read you, you earn from sponsors, paid subscriptions, and affiliate links (links where a company pays you a commission when a reader buys through them).

Think of it as a paper route in reverse. On a paper route, you deliver someone else's news for pennies. With a newsletter, you write the paper yourself, the houses *ask* to be on your route, and advertisers pay you to slip their flyer inside — because only you hold the keys to those mailboxes. No algorithm sits between you and your readers. That direct link is the whole business.

The main platforms in 2026:

- **Beehiiv** — free up to 2,500 subscribers, keeps 0% of your paid-subscription money, and has a built-in ad marketplace.
- **Substack** — free to start, but takes 10% of everything you earn from subscriptions.
- **Kit** (formerly ConvertKit) — popular with creators who also sell products.

## Why This Works in 2026

Inboxes are crowded: roughly 392 billion emails are projected to be sent daily in 2026, and AI makes generic newsletters cheap to pump out, so readers are pickier than ever.

Yet the money side has never been stronger. Substack passed 8.4 million paid subscriptions in early 2026 — up 68% from 5 million a year earlier — and nearly 100,000 publications now earn money there. Beehiiv's paid-subscription revenue jumped 138% in one year ($8M in 2024 to $19M in 2025). For newsletters launched in 2025, the median time to the **first dollar was 66 days**.

Why the boom? Readers drowning in machine-written sludge will pay for a trusted human filter, and the inbox — where *you* chose who gets in — is one of the last high-trust channels. Advertisers know it: a niche newsletter with 10,000 engaged readers can charge more per reader than a social account with a million followers. (A "niche" is one narrow topic served deeply.)

## How the Money Actually Flows

Three pipes, usually turned on in this order:

1. **Sponsorships/ads.** A company pays you to mention its product in your email. Beginners typically get $50-250 per placement. Pricing uses **CPM** — the cost per 1,000 readers — usually $10-30, though tight business niches command $50-100+. Beehiiv's Ad Network matches you with sponsors automatically once you qualify.
2. **Paid subscriptions.** Readers pay for extra content — the 2026 standard is $10/month or $100/year. The median newsletter converts only about **0.62%** of free readers into paying ones, so 1,000 free subscribers ≈ $62/month. Niche is everything: a 1,000-subscriber investing newsletter earns $2,700+/year, while a travel one earns about $252.
3. **Affiliate income.** You recommend tools you actually use; companies pay 10-50% commissions on resulting sales.

Extras come later: paid cross-promotions (Beehiiv "Boosts," where other newsletters pay you to recommend them), digital products, and consulting leads.

## What You Need to Start

- **Money:** $0. Beehiiv's free plan covers up to 2,500 subscribers; Substack is free with no limit. Budget $10-15/year for a custom domain — your own web address, which looks professional and protects deliverability (how reliably your emails land in inboxes, not spam). Paid plans run ~$49-89/month, needed only *after* you outgrow the free tiers — by then you should have income.
- **Skills:** clear writing (not fancy writing), real knowledge or deep curiosity about one narrow topic, and consistency. No coding.
- **Time:** 3-6 hours per week for a weekly issue. Do not go daily alone — creators who do consistently report burnout.
- **A niche where money already flows:** finance, B2B (business-to-business — content for people at work), AI/tech, and careers monetize far better than general lifestyle content.

## Your First 90 Days

- **Weeks 1-2:** Pick a niche you can write about for two years. Set up Beehiiv or Substack, write your welcome email, and publish issues #1-2 so the archive isn't empty.
- **Weeks 3-4:** Tell everyone you know. Post a useful excerpt from each issue on one social platform (LinkedIn or X for B2B niches). Goal: first 50 subscribers.
- **Weeks 5-8:** Publish every week, no exceptions. Ask 3-5 similar small newsletters to swap recommendations — free cross-promotion. Add one affiliate link where it genuinely fits. Goal: 200-500 subscribers.
- **Weeks 9-12:** Study which topics get opened and clicked; double down on those. Past ~500 engaged subscribers, apply to Beehiiv's Ad Network or email 5 small brands in your niche offering a $50-150 sponsorship slot — creators have landed paid ads with under 700 subscribers by showing strong engagement. Goal: 500-1,000 subscribers and your first dollar; the 66-day median says this is realistic.

## Realistic Earnings Timeline

- **Months 1-3:** $0-100. You are building the list. The median first dollar arrives around day 66, but many earn nothing yet — normal, not failure.
- **Months 4-6:** $50-300/month from small sponsorships and affiliates at 1,000-3,000 subscribers. Beehiiv reports its *average* user earns $20-150/month — most newsletters stay small.
- **Year 1+:** With 5,000-10,000 engaged subscribers in a good niche, $500-2,500/month is achievable by stacking sponsorships ($250-1,000 per slot at 10k subscribers), a paid tier, and affiliates. Sources disagree: optimistic guides claim $1,300-2,300/month at just 1,000 subscribers, while median data suggests more like $60-250/month at that size. Trust the median; treat the high numbers as best-case niches.
- **The ceiling is real:** 50+ Substack writers earn over $1M/year, and mid-tier examples like Extra Points generate ~$200,000/year. But those are outliers with years of work behind them.

## Will AI Kill This?

Partly — and that is the opportunity. AI can already write a generic "5 AI tools this week" email, so aggregated, personality-free newsletters are dying. Inbox tools that summarize emails weaken bland content further. What AI cannot fake over a 3-5 year horizon: your lived experience, your taste, your reporting (talking to real people), your track record of being right, and the trust that makes a reader pay $100/year. The winning 2026 formula: human judgment amplified by AI — use AI for research, editing, and social posts, but keep the opinions and stories human. Rating: 3/5 — safe as a voice-and-trust business, vulnerable as a summarizing business.

## Real-World Examples

- **Extra Points (Matt Brown):** a college-sports-business newsletter making around $200,000/year — narrow, unsexy niches beat broad ones.
- **Matt Navarra's Geekout:** a social-media-industry newsletter that earned $25,000 in one month just from Beehiiv Boosts (paid recommendations), on top of sponsorships.
- **Yaroslav Sobko (Cyber Corsairs):** turned small sponsored plugs into $65,000 over seven months — sponsorships work well before you are famous.
- **The failure side:** an estimated ~90% of newsletter creators quit within about three months, usually from unrealistic expectations and burnout — not bad writing.

## Common Mistakes Beginners Make

- Picking a broad topic ("productivity") instead of a narrow one ("productivity for nurses").
- Quitting at day 80 — right before the median first-dollar mark.
- Chasing subscriber count over engagement. Sponsors pay for open rates (the share of readers who open your email) and clicks, not vanity numbers.
- Going daily and burning out within weeks.
- Waiting for 10,000 subscribers when 500-2,000 engaged readers is enough to start monetizing.

## Pro Tips

- Pick a niche where readers have money and a problem: finance, careers, B2B, and AI newsletters monetize 5-10x better than lifestyle ones.
- Monetize embarrassingly early. A $50 sponsor at 600 subscribers teaches you the sales skill you will need at 10,000.
- Use Beehiiv if sponsorships are your plan (ad network, 0% cut); use Substack if paid subscriptions and its recommendation network fit better.
- Track clicks, not opens — Apple's privacy features inflate open rates by 15-20 points, and sponsors know it.
- Turn every issue into 3-5 social posts. Social is where subscribers come from; email is where money is made.
