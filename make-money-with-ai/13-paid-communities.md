# 13. Paid Communities & Memberships

> **Time to first income:** 1-6 months | **Startup cost:** $0-120 | **Beginner earnings:** $0-1,000/month | **Experienced earnings:** $2,800-15,000/month | **Difficulty:** 3/5 | **AI-Future-Proof:** 5/5

## What Is It, Really?

A paid community is a private online group. People pay a monthly fee to belong. Inside, they get discussions, live calls, courses, and — most important — each other. You pick a topic (fitness for new dads, freelance writing, real estate investing), gather people who care about it, and charge something like $29-49 per month.

Think of a gym. Nobody joins because they cannot do push-ups at home for free. They join for the equipment, the classes, and the feeling that others are showing up too. A paid community is a gym for a goal: your resources are the equipment, your live calls are the classes, and the other members keep everyone showing up.

The main platforms (websites that host your community) in 2026:

- **Skool** — simple, popular with coaches; $9-99/month to run.
- **Circle** — more polished; $89-199/month.
- **Discord** — a free chat app; to charge members it needs a *paywall* tool such as Whop (a paywall is software that blocks access until someone pays).

## Why This Works in 2026

AI gave community builders a strange gift: it made ordinary content nearly worthless. Analysts estimate AI-generated material could be around 90% of online content by 2026. Detected deepfake incidents (fake AI-made videos or images) jumped roughly 900% between 2023 and 2025, from about 500,000 to 8 million cases. When anyone can generate endless articles and videos, information stops being rare.

What stays rare is *verified humans*. You cannot fake a real person answering your specific question, celebrating your win, or honestly telling you your plan is bad. Trust researchers call this shift moving from "listen to us" to "watch how we behave together" — people want peer feedback, not more polished content.

The numbers agree. Average paid-subscriber *retention* (how long a paying member stays) rose from 2.8 months in 2024 to 4.2 months in 2025 — memberships are lasting longer as AI floods the open internet. Communities sell the one thing AI cannot make: belonging and accountability among real people.

## How the Money Actually Flows

1. **You create a community** on Skool, Circle, or Discord around a specific outcome: "pass the AWS exam," "land your first freelance client."
2. **Members pay a recurring subscription** — a fee charged every month, typically $26-50. Roughly a third of all communities price in that range. The platform takes a cut: Skool 10% on the cheap plan and 2.9% on Pro, Circle 1-2%, Discord's native payments about 16%.
3. **Recurring revenue compounds.** This is *MRR* — monthly recurring revenue, money that arrives every month without a new sale. 100 members at $39 is $3,900 MRR.
4. **You lose members to churn** — the percentage who cancel each month. Average Skool churn is around 18%; under 10% is good. Churn is the silent tax on this business.
5. **Advanced owners add extra offers:** paid challenges, cohort courses (a group starts and finishes together), and coaching. Skool research shows top earners make $150-250 per member per month by stacking these, versus a $40-60 baseline from subscriptions alone.

## What You Need to Start

- **A niche where people already spend money.** A *niche* is a narrow, specific topic. Pick one tied to a painful, ongoing goal. "General self-improvement" fails; "Etsy sellers trying to hit $1K/month" works.
- **Some audience, or a plan to build one.** This is the honest gatekeeper. With 3,000+ social followers, first revenue typically comes in 30-45 days. With zero audience, expect 90-180 days of free content and networking first.
- **Money:** $9/month for Skool Hobby, or about $0 with Discord plus a paywall tool that takes a percentage. Budget $0-120 for the first two months.
- **Skills:** steady writing or speaking about your topic, hosting live calls, and personally welcoming every early member. No coding needed.
- **Time:** 5-10 hours per week minimum. A community dies when ignored.

## Your First 90 Days

- **Weeks 1-2:** Pick your niche and outcome. Interview 5-10 target people about their biggest struggle. Start the community *free* — free removes friction while you learn.
- **Weeks 3-4:** Invite 20-50 people personally through direct messages (private one-to-one messages), not mass posts. Post 3+ times weekly, run one live call, answer every question.
- **Weeks 5-6:** Run a free 3-day challenge — a short guided sprint toward a small win. Watch who takes part most.
- **Weeks 7-9:** Launch your paid tier at $29-49/month to your most engaged free members — Skool data shows challenge finishers convert to paid at notably higher rates than cold prospects. Aim for 5-10 paying members.
- **Weeks 10-13:** Deliver hard: weekly calls, member spotlights, fast answers. Ask every member what almost stopped them joining, and fix it. Keep publishing free content outside the group to attract new people.

## Realistic Earnings Timeline

- **Months 1-3:** $0-500/month. Many earn exactly $0 while building a free group — normal, not failure. First income lands between week 4 (with an existing audience) and month 6 (with none).
- **Months 4-6:** $200-1,000/month. Benchmarks across 100,000+ paid subscriptions found the median paying creator earns about $1,000/month from ~26 members at ~$39/month — your honest six-to-twelve-month target.
- **Year 1+:** $1,000-5,000/month is a solid outcome. Median MRR for established paid Skool communities is roughly $2,800-4,200/month. The top 10% exceed $25,000/month, usually by adding cohorts and coaching — outliers, not expectations. And 44% of communities have under 100 members; small can still be profitable.

## Will AI Kill This?

This is one of the most AI-resistant models in this guide — arguably *strengthened* by AI. The product is human connection: accountability, peer feedback, status, friendship. AI can generate a course in an hour — exactly why courses are collapsing in price and why 2026 creator-economy reports call memberships the primary revenue foundation for community-led creators.

Two warnings. First, if your community's real value is information (your posts, your templates), AI will erode it — members can ask a chatbot instead. Stay valuable by making the *members* the product: introductions, live events, member-run wins channels. Second, low barriers mean rising competition. Skool's hype cycle, fueled by Alex Hormozi's "Skool Games" competition, has pulled in thousands of new creators. Generic "make money online" communities are badly saturated; specific, unglamorous niches are not.

## Real-World Examples

- **Tiger 21**, an exclusive peer group for wealthy investors, earns an estimated $40M/year from only 1,200 members. Extreme, but proof that people pay huge sums for the *right room*, not for content.
- **Nick Pauly** documented on Medium getting his first paid Skool member in under 14 days, using an existing warm audience — the pattern behind most "fast" wins.
- **Impromptu Speakers**, a public-speaking community, left Skool for Circle because the platform could not support its training structure. Platform choice matters, and communities do fail or move.

## Common Mistakes Beginners Make

- Launching paid on day one with no proof anyone wants in.
- Choosing a broad, passionless niche because it "seems profitable."
- Treating it as passive income. Ignored communities become ghost towns, and 18% average churn eats them alive.
- Competing on content volume — the one game AI always wins.
- Underpricing at $5-10/month, which attracts unengaged members and cannot pay for your time.

## Pro Tips

- Fix retention before growth. Cutting churn from 18% to 9% roughly doubles your long-term size at the same effort.
- Personally welcome every new member within 48 hours — the single biggest retention lever.
- Engineer member-to-member wins. When members would miss *each other*, not just you, cancelling feels like leaving friends.
- Add one paid challenge or cohort per quarter once you pass ~50 members. That is how median communities become top earners.
- Keep posting free content publicly forever; discovery still happens outside the community.
