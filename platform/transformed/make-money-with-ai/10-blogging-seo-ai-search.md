For fifteen years, Google was a busy street sending walkers past your shop window. Then, around 2024 to 2026, a giant mall of AI answers opened at the street's entrance, and most shoppers stopped walking the street at all. To blog for money in 2026, you have to accept that the old "free traffic from Google" business is badly wounded, and build for the world that replaced it.

> **Time to first income:** 3-6 months (median 12 months to first dollar) | **Startup cost:** $50-150 | **Beginner earnings:** $0-100/month | **Experienced earnings:** $500-3,000/month | **Difficulty:** 4/5 | **AI-proof:** 2/5

## What niche blogging actually is

A **niche blog** is a website where you write often about one narrow subject - sourdough baking, budget travel in Portugal, home aquariums - and earn money from the readers it attracts. **SEO (Search Engine Optimization)** is the craft of arranging your pages so search engines like Google show them to people looking for those topics.

The shops that survived the mall opening shared one habit: they collected customers' contact details and could invite people back directly. That is the whole story of blogging in 2026. The winning asset is a *direct audience* - mainly an **email list**, meaning readers who handed you their email address so you can reach them anytime.

## The bad news, stated plainly

**AI Overviews** - the AI-written summaries Google now shows above its results - appear on over 25% of searches. When one shows up, roughly 80% of those searches end with *no click to any website at all*. Ahrefs, an SEO research company, measured a 58% drop in clicks to the #1 ranked page, and publishers overall saw Google traffic fall about 38% in 2025.

Real sites felt it hard. **Charleston Crafted**, an established home improvement blog, lost roughly 70 to 74% of its traffic within months of AI Overviews rolling out, and about 65% of its ad revenue. **HouseFresh**, an air purifier review site famous for actually testing products, lost about 91% of its Google traffic after 2024's updates and documented Google's AI summarizing its testing without sending a single reader over. Even genuine expertise did not protect its *traffic*.

## Why it still works for the right people

So why start? Three things survive, backed by 2026 data.

- **First-hand experience.** AI can summarize what already exists online, but it cannot test five air purifiers in its living room or hike a trail and photograph it. Content only a real human could produce still ranks and gets cited.
- **Email.** The 2026 Blogging Income Survey from Productive Blogging found that bloggers who do email marketing earn **twice as much** as those who do not. It is the audience you own.
- **Digital products** - things you make once and sell many times, like ebooks, templates, and courses. The same survey found they earn about **$536 per 1,000 views**, versus about **$33** from ads. That is 16 times more, so you need far less traffic to make real money.

> Treat the blog as the front door and the email list as the store. Measure success in subscribers, not pageviews.

One emerging wrinkle is **GEO (Generative Engine Optimization)**: getting your site *cited* inside AI answers on ChatGPT and Perplexity. AI-referred visits grew over 500% year-on-year in 2025 and reportedly convert to sales at higher rates. It is tiny today - only 1% of surveyed bloggers called it their biggest driver - but it is the direction of travel.

## How the money actually flows

There are five main streams; the smart move is to weight them away from the ones AI hurt most.

1. **Display ads.** Networks like Mediavine or Raptive place ads on your pages and pay per view, roughly $10 to $40 per 1,000 views. To join in 2026, Mediavine's "Journey" tier needs about 1,000 sessions a month and Raptive needs 25,000 pageviews a month. This is the stream AI hurt most.
2. **Affiliate links.** A company pays a commission, often 3 to 30% of the sale, when a reader buys through your tracking link. Also hurt, because AI now answers "best X" questions directly.
3. **Digital products.** Your own ebook, template pack, or mini-course, sold mostly through your email list. This is the most AI-resistant stream, and it overlaps neatly with [digital products](/blog/make-money-with-ai/11-selling-digital-products) as a business of its own.
4. **Sponsorships.** Brands pay to appear in your posts or newsletter, roughly $15 to $50 per 1,000 subscribers per email send.
5. **Services.** Freelancing or coaching sold on your blog's credibility. This is often the *fastest* route to your first dollar.

## What you need to start

You do not need much money, but you do need patience.

- **Money:** $10 to $15 a year for a domain name (your web address), $5 to $10 a month for hosting (the computer that serves your site), free WordPress software, and a free email tool tier like Kit or MailerLite. Total: **$50 to $150 for year one.**
- **Skills:** [clear writing](/tools/readability-grade-level-scorer), basic keyword research (finding what people type into search, free with Google's own tools), and real knowledge of your subject - or the will to *go do things* in your niche so you have first-hand experience.
- **Time and temperament:** 8 to 15 hours a week for a year before meaningful money arrives. This is among the slowest opportunities in [the full 2026 guide](/blog/make-money-with-ai/00-index-make-money-with-ai), so expect a marathon.

## Your first 90 days

- **Weeks 1-2:** Pick a niche where you have real experience and where people spend money. Buy a domain, set up WordPress, and write an "about" page explaining why you are credible.
- **Weeks 3-6:** Publish 8 to 10 articles answering questions AI cannot answer well - personal tests, local knowledge, original photos. Add an email signup box with a small freebie from day one.
- **Weeks 7-10:** Publish 8 to 10 more posts, all linked around one core topic. This builds **topical authority**, meaning search engines trust you as the go-to source on that subject. Start one non-Google channel too: Pinterest, YouTube, or Reddit and Facebook communities.
- **Weeks 11-13:** Join 2 to 3 relevant affiliate programs. Send your first weekly email, even to just 20 subscribers, and treat it as the seed of [a newsletter](/blog/make-money-with-ai/09-newsletter-business) you will grow for years. Then double down on whatever gets traffic.

## A realistic earnings timeline

- **Months 1-3:** $0. Google takes months to trust a new site.
- **Months 4-6:** $0 to $100 a month, usually a first affiliate commission or a service client. Most people quit right here.
- **Months 7-12:** $50 to $500 a month with steady publishing (50 or more good posts) and a growing email list. The 2026 survey's *median* blogger took 12 months to earn a first dollar; the average was 21 months.
- **Year 1-2:** $300 to $1,500 a month is a realistic good outcome, and $500 to $3,000 a month for those who add digital products. The survey's average time to full-time income was about 4 years, and most bloggers never get there. Treat any "$10k a month in 6 months" claim as pure marketing.

## Will AI kill this?

Partly, it already has. The old traffic game - write generic articles, rank, collect ad money - is dead and not coming back, and Google clicks will likely keep shrinking over the next 3 to 5 years.

What survives are creators who are a *source*, not a middleman: original data, tested products, lived experience, a recognizable name, and an owned email audience. Oddly, AI search may reward exactly this, because AI systems need credible primary sources to cite, and cited brands get high-intent visitors. **Living The Dream**, a travel blog, publicly reported $8,500 in May 2026 - $3,500 from ads, $2,545 from sponsorships, and $2,000 from affiliates - proving a diversified blog can still earn well. The old model rates 1 out of 5 for AI-resistance and the email-plus-products-plus-expertise model a 3, blending to a 2. Go in with eyes open.

## The bottom line

Niche blogging in 2026 is slower and harder than the internet's old promises suggest, and the traffic-for-ads model that made it famous is dying. But the front-door-to-a-store version still works for people who write what only they could write and turn readers into subscribers. **Stop chasing pageviews and build an owned email audience that buys your products - that is the only blogging business AI has not broken.**
