# 18. Online Tutoring & Coaching (incl. Teaching AI Skills)

> **Time to first income:** 2-6 weeks | **Startup cost:** $0-300 | **Beginner earnings:** $200-1,000/month | **Experienced earnings:** $2,000-8,000/month | **Difficulty:** 2/5 | **AI-Future-Proof:** 4/5

## What Is It, Really?
Online tutoring means teaching a skill or school subject over a video call — math, English, coding, or test prep (helping students prepare for exams like the SAT). Increasingly, it also means teaching people to use AI tools like ChatGPT. "Coaching" is the same idea for adults: helping a marketing manager use AI at work, or a retiree understand what this "ChatGPT thing" actually does.

Think of a personal trainer. Free workout videos have existed for decades, yet people still pay trainers $50-100 an hour, because a trainer watches *you*, fixes *your* mistakes, and makes you show up. Tutoring is that, for the brain.

Two ways to run this business:
1. **Platforms** — websites like Wyzant or Preply that connect tutors with students. They find customers but take a cut of every lesson.
2. **Independent** — you find your own clients, keep 100% of the fee, and do your own marketing.

## Why This Works in 2026
Two booms feed this field.

First, normal tutoring keeps growing: the global online market went from roughly $10.4 billion in 2025 to a projected $12.8 billion in 2026, and analysts expect the U.S. market to grow by about $28 billion between 2025 and 2029. Parents still pay $100+ per hour for SAT prep tutors with good track records.

Second, and newer: **AI skills training**. Research firm IDC projects that over 90% of large companies face AI skills shortages in 2026, and the World Economic Forum estimates 39% of core job skills will change by 2030. Millions of workers feel behind and will pay a patient person to catch them up. Trainers in 2025-2026 report charging $300-600 for a two-hour "ChatGPT for your team" workshop, and $2,000-5,000 for half-day corporate sessions. A quieter niche (a small, focused market): teaching AI basics to older adults. Senior Planet and OpenAI's own "AI for Older Adults" events show real demand, and courses like "ChatGPT for Seniors" already sell via Zoom.

You do not need to be a programmer to teach AI — only six months ahead of your student.

## How the Money Actually Flows
- **Platform tutoring:** A student books and pays through a site like Preply or Wyzant. The platform pays you minus a commission (a percentage fee it keeps). Preply takes 33% at first, dropping to 18% after 400 taught hours; across platforms, expect 20-40%. Money arrives by PayPal or bank transfer, usually weekly or after each lesson.
- **Independent tutoring:** A parent or professional pays you directly (Venmo, Stripe, bank transfer), per hour or as a prepaid package of 5-10 sessions. Packages are better: money upfront, fewer no-shows.
- **AI workshops:** A business or community group pays one flat fee for a group session. One payment, many listeners — a far higher hourly rate than one-on-one work.

## What You Need to Start
- **A skill you are truly better at than your target student.** For school subjects, strong high-school math or English is enough. For AI coaching: 2-3 months of serious daily use of ChatGPT or Claude, plus real examples of work you improved with it.
- **Tools ($0-100):** a laptop with a webcam, free Zoom or Google Meet, a quiet room, and a free scheduling tool like Calendly. A $50-80 headset makes you sound more professional.
- **Optional ($0-200):** a ChatGPT Plus or Claude subscription (~$20/month) if teaching AI; a one-page website ($0-100/year).
- **Credentials:** no license is needed for most subjects (unlike, say, therapy). For test prep, a strong score on the test you teach is your credential.

Total realistic startup: **$0-300**.

## Your First 90 Days
- **Weeks 1-2:** Pick ONE niche — high-school algebra, SAT math, "ChatGPT for real-estate agents," or "AI basics for seniors." Write a one-paragraph pitch. Sign up on one platform (Preply usually approves profiles within about 5 business days) AND tell 20 people what you offer.
- **Weeks 3-4:** Price low — $15-25/hour on platforms — because new profiles need bookings, not high rates. Give 2-3 free or half-price sessions to friends-of-friends in exchange for written testimonials (quotes from happy students).
- **Weeks 5-8:** Teach everyone who books. After each good session, ask for a review and a referral. If teaching AI, offer a free 45-minute lunch-and-learn (a casual talk over lunch) to a local business, library, or senior center — your live audition.
- **Weeks 9-12:** Raise rates 20-30% once you have 5+ reviews. Turn hourly clients into 5-session packages. Pitch your first *paid* workshop ($200-400) to a group that saw the free one.

## Realistic Earnings Timeline
- **Months 1-3:** $0-500/month. The first booking is the hardest. Some Preply tutors report waiting months — one reported nearly a year — while others with sharp profiles and low intro rates book within 1-2 weeks. Platform commissions (up to 33%) shrink these early earnings further.
- **Months 4-6:** $500-1,500/month part-time. Typical platform tutors average around $26/hour (Wyzant's reported average is $26.79); 10-15 hours a week gets you here. AI-workshop sellers report first-month revenues of $500-2,000 by pitching local businesses.
- **Year 1+:** $2,000-8,000/month is realistic at 15-25 hours weekly, mostly off platforms. Independent test-prep and STEM tutors charge $50-120/hour; business AI coaching runs $75-150/hour. The famous outliers earning $1,000/hour are rare and took years. Ignore anyone promising $10K/month in 90 days.

## Will AI Kill This?
Partly — so pick your lane. AI tutors like Khan Academy's Khanmigo (2 million users) explain concepts patiently, 24/7, and cost 40-60% less per student than humans. Basic "explain this concept" tutoring is the most exposed segment and will keep shrinking in price.

But research, including Brookings' review of generative-AI tutoring, finds AI works best as a *supplement*. It cannot make a discouraged 14-year-old show up, build trust with an anxious parent, or hold a retiree's hand through their first prompt. Motivation, accountability, and relationships are your moat (your protection from competition). Ironically, AI created the fastest-growing tutoring subject: *itself*. McKinsey estimates 20-40% of a tutor's prep and admin time can be automated — use AI to prepare lessons faster and you become cheaper to run, not obsolete. Verdict: 4/5 future-proof *if* you sell accountability and AI skills; 2/5 if you sell generic homework help.

## Real-World Examples
- **Steven Menking (reported by CNBC, 2025):** left a six-figure Wall Street job to tutor. He started at $50-100/hour; after years of referrals in high-end test prep and academics, he now reportedly earns $1,000/hour working 20-25 hours a week from home. An outlier built on reputation, not a starting point.
- **AI workshop trainers (documented in 2025-2026 side-hustle reports):** selling a "get 10x better results from ChatGPT" two-hour team workshop at $300-600 per session, with corporate half-day training at $2,000-5,000. Startup cost: a $20/month subscription plus preparation.
- **A cautionary Preply data point:** at least one tutor reported taking close to a year to get steady students, because Preply's algorithm favors profiles with 90+ profile scores and 30+ hours of weekly availability. Platforms are crowded; slow starts are common.

## Common Mistakes Beginners Make
- Staying on platforms forever, giving away 20-33% of income instead of building direct clients.
- Pricing high with zero reviews — new profiles need social proof first.
- Teaching "everything" instead of one findable niche.
- Selling knowledge instead of outcomes ("I know calculus" vs. "I raise SAT math scores 100+ points").
- In AI coaching: reading slides at people instead of making them practice on *their own* real work.

## Pro Tips
- Sell 5- or 10-session packages, paid upfront. It steadies income and improves results.
- Group workshops are the earnings cheat code: 10 people at $40 each beats one person at $60.
- Post short answer-videos on YouTube or TikTok — free marketing that pre-sells your teaching style.
- For AI coaching, niche by *profession* ("AI for accountants"), not by tool. Tools change monthly; professions don't.
- Use AI to cut lesson-prep time; spend the saved time on the human parts no app can do.
