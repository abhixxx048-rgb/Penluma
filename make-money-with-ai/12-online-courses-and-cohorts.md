# 12. Online Courses & Cohort Programs

> **Time to first income:** 2-4 months | **Startup cost:** $0-300 | **Beginner earnings:** $0-1,000/month | **Experienced earnings:** $3,000-15,000/month | **Difficulty:** 4/5 | **AI-Future-Proof:** 3/5

## What Is It, Really?

An **online course** is a set of pre-recorded video lessons (plus worksheets or quizzes) that people pay to watch. You record once and sell many times. A **cohort program** (or cohort-based course) works more like a class: students start together on a fixed date, meet on live video calls, get your feedback, and finish together.

Free YouTube workout videos teach exercise, yet people pay $60 an hour for a trainer who watches their form, fixes mistakes, and makes them show up. The course is the video; the cohort is the trainer. In 2026, free AI crushed the video side; money moved to the trainer side.

## Why This Works in 2026

Roughly 900 million people use ChatGPT every week; by some estimates about 60% use it to learn something. Every buyer now asks: "Why pay $300 for a course when I can ask AI for free?"

The honest answer: information-only courses are dying; **transformation** courses — ones that change what the student can do — are not. Udemy, the biggest marketplace for cheap self-paced courses, cut instructor payouts on its subscription product from 25% to 15% between 2023 and 2026; some long-time instructors report revenue drops of 30-67% since AI took off. Yet the industry passed roughly $300 billion, and cohort courses on platforms like Maven sell for an average of about $2,500 per student.

Free **MOOCs** (massive open online courses) have completion rates of 5-15%; cohort courses report 70-90%. People no longer pay for information — AI gives it away. They pay for accountability, feedback on their own work, community, and a teacher who has done the thing. Creators who include a community earn roughly twice as much as those who sell videos alone.

## How the Money Actually Flows

1. **Pick a specific outcome** you can teach — "pass the AWS exam in 6 weeks" or "land your first freelance client" — not a broad topic.
2. **Build a small audience** — people who trust you on this topic — via an **email list** (people who opted in to your emails), YouTube, or LinkedIn.
3. **Sell before you build.** Announce the course, take payments via **Stripe** (a payment processor that handles card payments) or a platform's checkout, and record content only after people pay.
4. **Students pay you directly:** $50-300 for a self-paced course, $500-3,000 for a cohort. On your own site you keep about 90-97% after payment fees; Maven keeps 10%; Udemy keeps most of the price — avoid it as your main channel.
5. **Deliver, improve, relaunch.** Collect **testimonials** (short statements from happy students) and fix weak spots. **Profit margins** — the share of each sale you keep — run 70-90% because there is no inventory.

Audience size and trust, not course quality, predict income. Real launches: a fitness creator with 900 email subscribers sold a $99 course and made $3,115 (3.5% of the list bought); a business coach with 2,400 subscribers made about $30,000 on a first launch at a higher price. No audience means no sales, no matter how good the course is.

## What You Need to Start

- **A skill with proof:** be a few steps ahead of your students, with results — yours or your clients'.
- **Basic gear:** a phone or webcam, a $30-100 USB microphone, and free screen-recording software (OBS). Audio matters more than video.
- **A platform:** Maven or a free Teachable/Podia tier ($0), Zoom for live calls (free tier works), and a free email tool like MailerLite. Total realistic startup: $0-300.
- **Marketing stamina:** post content and talk to students for 2-3 months before you see a dollar.
- **AI helpers:** ChatGPT or Claude for outlines and worksheets, Descript for editing. AI cuts production time roughly in half — but also for your competitors, so speed is no longer an advantage.

## Your First 90 Days

- **Weeks 1-2:** Pick one narrow outcome for one specific type of person. Interview 5-10 people in that group on free calls and write down their exact words about the problem.
- **Weeks 3-6:** Post helpful content 3-4 times a week where those people gather (LinkedIn, YouTube, Reddit). Start an email list with a free downloadable resource. Goal: 100-300 subscribers.
- **Weeks 7-8:** Outline a 4-6 week cohort. Write a one-page **sales page** — a web page that explains the course and takes payment. Price the first run at $150-500 — cheap because unproven.
- **Weeks 9-10:** **Pre-sell** — sell seats before the course exists — to your list and network. Target 5-15 founding students. If fewer than 5 buy, your offer or audience is wrong — fix it before recording anything.
- **Weeks 11-13:** Run the cohort live over Zoom and over-deliver. Collect detailed testimonials and note every student question — your curriculum for version 2.

## Realistic Earnings Timeline

- **Months 1-3:** Usually $0. A small founding cohort might bring $750-3,000 total (5-15 students at $150-300). Many first launches make nothing.
- **Months 4-6:** With testimonials and a growing list, $500-3,000/month is realistic. Sources report new creators reaching $500-5,000/month "once they get traction" — and many never do.
- **Year 1+:** Steady creators commonly report $1,000-10,000/month. Kajabi says its average creator earns about $37,000/year — but its fees filter for serious creators. Udemy's average instructor earns roughly $3,300/year — what marketplace-only strategies pay. Six figures is possible but usually takes 2+ years and a real audience.

## Will AI Kill This?

Partially — it already killed the bottom tier. Over the next 3-5 years, expect generic "how to" self-paced courses to keep collapsing: AI tutors answer those questions free, instantly, and personally. Udemy-style marketplaces will keep squeezing instructors.

What survives: courses selling accountability, live feedback, community, credentials, and access to a practitioner with real-world scars. AI can explain piano theory, but it cannot watch your hands or make you show up on Tuesday. To stay valuable: teach from documented personal experience, run live cohorts, grade real student work, and build a community students would miss. If a good ChatGPT conversation could replace your course, it will.

## Real-World Examples

- **Jacques Hopkins (Piano in 21 Days):** his piano course still "consistently makes five figures a month" in 2026 — a non-saturated **niche** (narrow topic area; "saturated" means overcrowded) where video demonstration beats AI text.
- **Amy Porterfield (Digital Course Academy):** a famous "course about courses" was shut down despite being reportedly profitable, because it depended on increasingly expensive paid ads — a sign the teach-online-marketing niche is saturated.
- **Udemy instructors:** one instructor with 80,000 students and 60+ courses publicly reported a 30-50% revenue drop after AI's rise; Udemy's own payout rate fell 40% in three years. Relying on a marketplace you do not control is the failure pattern.

## Common Mistakes Beginners Make

- Recording the whole course before selling a single seat.
- Choosing a broad topic ("productivity") instead of a specific outcome for a specific person.
- Launching to an audience of zero and blaming the course.
- Competing on information (AI wins) instead of transformation and feedback.
- Pricing at $19 on Udemy and expecting a living wage.

## Pro Tips

- Pre-sell everything — money in the bank is the only real validation.
- Charge more, serve fewer: 10 students at $500 beats 100 at $50 — and far less support work.
- Own your email list — platforms and algorithms change; your list does not.
- Turn every student question into content and every result into a testimonial with numbers.
- Add a community or live component early — the data says it roughly doubles earnings.
