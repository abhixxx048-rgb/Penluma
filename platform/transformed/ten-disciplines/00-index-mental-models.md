Smart people rarely fail for lack of information. They fail because they reach for the one idea they know best and force every problem to fit it. The economist sees incentives everywhere, the engineer sees systems, the psychologist sees bias; each is right about a slice and blind to the rest.

**A cross-disciplinary thinking toolkit is a collection of the biggest, most reusable ideas from many fields, held loosely enough that you can pick the right one for the problem in front of you.** These reusable ideas are called mental models: compact explanations of how some corner of the world works, like feedback loops, opportunity cost, or expected value. This hub maps thirty of them across ten disciplines, so you can build what Charlie Munger called a "latticework" of models instead of a single hammer that makes everything look like a nail.

## Who this is for

This is for the curious generalist who wants to understand a bit of everything well enough to think clearly, argue honestly, and decide better. Founders, students, managers, writers, and anyone rebuilding their education on their own terms will feel at home here.

Do not read this like a textbook, front to back. It is a **reference library, not a course.** Skim the roadmap below, notice which discipline speaks to a problem you actually have, and start there. If you want a guided on-ramp, the "Start here" list gives you the guides that pay off fastest.

## Why a thinking toolkit matters

Think of your mind as a workshop. If the only tool on the wall is a hammer, every job becomes a nail, and you spend your life bending screws. Add a screwdriver, a saw, and a level, and you can build almost anything, because you match the tool to the task instead of the task to the tool.

That is the whole promise here. Systems thinking teaches you to see structure, economics to see trade-offs, psychology to see your own blind spots. Together they cover each other's gaps. The magic is not mastering any one field, it is having enough range that the right idea shows up when you need it.

## The full roadmap

Every guide is below, grouped into five clusters of related disciplines. Each cluster moves from a plain-English foundation to a more advanced, applied guide.

- [Stocks, Flows, Feedback Loops: How Every System Works](/blog/ten-disciplines/02-the-machinery-of-systems-stocks-flows-feedback-loops-and-delays)  ::  The moving parts behind everything from your bank balance to the climate, and why delays fool everyone.
- [Leverage Points: Where to Push to Actually Change a System](/blog/ten-disciplines/03-leverage-points-system-traps-and-thinking-in-systems-advanced-real-world)  ::  Most fixes push where the system barely moves; learn where small effort creates real change.
- [How Prices Secretly Carry Information (Economics 101)](/blog/ten-disciplines/04-scarcity-choice-and-how-prices-carry-information-micro-foundations)  ::  Scarcity, opportunity cost, and how one price carries knowledge no single person holds.

## The bottom line

You do not need a genius-level IQ to think well. You need a handful of sturdy ideas from many fields and the habit of reaching for the right one. Collect a few models from each discipline here and you will out-think far smarter people who only own a hammer. **Range beats raw horsepower: ten decent tools quietly outperform one great one.**
