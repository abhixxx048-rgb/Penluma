Most people think economics is stock charts, interest rates, and a language they were never taught to speak. So they tune it out - and then feel lost every time the news mentions inflation, a recession, or the national debt. It does not have to be that way.

**To learn economics from first principles, you start with one fact - that people can never have everything they want - and rebuild every other idea on top of it, in plain English, with no jargon and no prior knowledge assumed.** This course is that rebuild: 32 short guides that take you from "what even is economics" to how the whole global system connects.

## Who this is for

This is for the curious beginner. If you have never taken an economics class - or took one and remember nothing but supply-and-demand doodles - you are exactly who this was written for. There is no math beyond arithmetic, and every term gets defined the moment it appears.

Use it two ways. **Read it in order** the first time, because the early guides build the vocabulary the later ones rely on - you cannot understand central banks until you understand what money is. After that, treat it as a **reference shelf**: jump straight to the guide you need when the news throws a word at you.

## Why economics matters

Here is the payoff. Economics is not a subject about money - it is a lens for how people choose when they cannot have everything. Once you own that lens, the world stops looking random.

Think of an economy like weather. From the ground it feels chaotic - prices jump, jobs vanish, currencies swing. But weather runs on a few forces you can learn: pressure, temperature, moisture. An economy is the same. A handful of forces - scarcity, incentives, supply and demand, trust - drive almost everything, and once you can name them, you read the forecast instead of just getting rained on. That same wiring of choice shows up in [the psychology of decisions](/blog/psychology-of-decisions/00-index-decision-psychology), where the pressures are personal rather than national.

## The full roadmap

Here is the whole course, grouped into five tiers that build on each other. Read top to bottom, or dip in wherever you need.

### Tier 1: The foundations - how economists think

Start here. These four guides install the mental toolkit everything else depends on.

- [What Is Economics? The One Idea That Explains It All](/blog/economics-from-first-principles/01-what-economics-is-and-why-it-exists) - Economics isn't really about money. It's about how people choose when they can't have everything. This is the one idea that makes the whole field click.
- [How Economists Think: Incentives, Margins, and Models](/blog/economics-from-first-principles/02-how-economists-think-incentives-margins-and-models) - The mental moves economists use every day: following incentives, thinking at the margin, and modeling a messy world.
- [Supply and Demand: How Prices Really Get Set](/blog/economics-from-first-principles/03-supply-demand-and-how-prices-are-determined) - Nobody decides what your coffee costs, yet the price is never random. Here is how supply and demand quietly set it.
- [Why Markets Fail: Monopolies, Pollution, and the Limits of Competition](/blog/economics-from-first-principles/04-markets-competition-and-when-markets-fail) - Markets usually serve us well, but four classic failures break them: monopolies, externalities, public goods, and information gaps.

### Tier 2: Money, banks, and the machinery of value

Now that you can think like an economist, meet the strangest invention in the story: money, and the institutions that create and steer it.

- [How People Really Decide What to Buy](/blog/economics-from-first-principles/05-consumer-behavior-and-how-people-really-decide) - Why does cheap water cost nothing while diamonds cost a fortune? How people really decide, and how sellers use it.
- [What Money Is and Why It Has Value (The Real Answer)](/blog/economics-from-first-principles/06-what-money-is-and-why-it-has-value) - Why does a slip of paper buy real food and labor? What money actually is, and what happens when trust in it breaks.
- [How Do Banks Create Money? The Truth About Your Deposits](/blog/economics-from-first-principles/07-how-banks-work-and-how-they-create-money) - Banks don't lend out your savings the way you think. They create new money every time they make a loan. Here is how that really works.
- [How Central Banks Quietly Steer the Whole Economy](/blog/economics-from-first-principles/08-central-banks-and-monetary-policy) - How central banks move one interest rate to steer jobs, prices, mortgages, and the dollar - and why it takes a year to reach your life.
- [Interest Rates Explained: How Loans and Debt Really Work](/blog/economics-from-first-principles/09-loans-credit-debt-and-interest-rates) - Why interest exists, how your credit score sets your rate, and why real returns beat the number on your statement.
- [Inflation and Deflation: Why Your Money Keeps Losing Value](/blog/economics-from-first-principles/10-inflation-deflation-and-the-value-of-money) - Why a coffee costs ten times what it did in 1980. Inflation, deflation, and the value of money, without the jargon.

### Tier 3: Reading the whole economy

Zoom out from single choices to the big numbers - the gauges economists watch to tell whether a country is thriving or stalling.

- [GDP, CPI, PPP Explained: How to Actually Read an Economy](/blog/economics-from-first-principles/11-measuring-an-economy-gdp-gnp-cpi-ppp-and-key-indicators) - What GDP, GNP, CPI, and PPP each measure, what they quietly hide, and how to read an economy.
- [Why Are Some Countries Rich? The Math Behind Growth](/blog/economics-from-first-principles/12-economic-growth-and-productivity) - Why some nations are rich. The Rule of 70, the Solow model, and why institutions matter most.
- [Why Some Jobs Pay More: The Real Reason Behind Your Wage](/blog/economics-from-first-principles/13-wages-labor-markets-and-why-some-jobs-pay-more) - Not effort or virtue. The six forces that set every wage, from skill scarcity to superstar markets.
- [How to Actually Read the Unemployment Rate](/blog/economics-from-first-principles/14-unemployment-productivity-and-the-labor-force) - The rate can fall while nobody gets hired. How it is counted, what it hides, and how to read the jobs report.
- [The Business Cycle: Why Booms Always End in Busts](/blog/economics-from-first-principles/15-the-business-cycle-booms-recessions-and-depressions) - The four phases, what causes booms and busts, and the warning signs that a downturn is coming.
- [Deficit vs Debt: What the $37 Trillion Really Means](/blog/economics-from-first-principles/16-fiscal-policy-taxes-spending-deficits-and-national-debt) - How taxes, spending, deficits, and the debt work, why the debt isn't like your mortgage, and what really limits it.

### Tier 4: Business, markets, and where money flows

Follow the money into the real world - firms, investors, houses, and the markets that price the future.

- [Why a Software Firm Keeps 70 Cents and a Grocer Keeps 2](/blog/economics-from-first-principles/17-business-economics-costs-profit-competition-and-pricing) - How costs, margins, pricing, and moats decide which firms win - and why the same iPhone costs more abroad.
- [How Companies Grow, Raise Capital, and Fund Your Raise](/blog/economics-from-first-principles/18-how-companies-grow-raise-capital-and-create-value) - From bootstrapping to VC, dilution, and the chain that turns one investment into higher wages.
- [Stocks, Bonds, and Derivatives Explained Simply](/blog/economics-from-first-principles/19-financial-markets-stocks-bonds-commodities-and-derivatives) - How markets price the future, move risk around, and reward patience over panic.
- [Housing Economics: Why Cheap Mortgages Cost You More](/blog/economics-from-first-principles/20-real-estate-and-housing-economics) - Why house prices rise, how rates move them, the real rent-vs-buy math, and what caused the 2008 crash.

### Tier 5: The connected world and its big questions

Finally, zoom all the way out to the global system - trade, crises, inequality, technology - and the forces shaping the decades ahead.

- [Why AI Won't Take Your Job (But Will Reshape It)](/blog/economics-from-first-principles/27-technology-automation-ai-and-innovation) - History says automation reinvents work more than it erases it. How technology drives growth and jobs.
- [How Population and Demographics Shape Every Economy](/blog/economics-from-first-principles/28-population-demographics-and-the-economy) - Why age and fertility rates quietly decide whether an economy grows or stalls.
- [Why Government Exists: The Economics of When Markets Fail](/blog/economics-from-first-principles/29-public-economics-welfare-social-security-and-the-role-of-government) - When government should step in, how it taxes, and why Social Security is at risk - plus the Laffer curve and UBI.
