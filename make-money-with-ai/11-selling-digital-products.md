# 11. Selling Digital Products

> **Time to first income:** 2-8 weeks | **Startup cost:** $0-100 | **Beginner earnings:** $0-300/month | **Experienced earnings:** $500-3,000/month | **Difficulty:** 2/5 | **AI-Future-Proof:** 2/5

## What Is It, Really?

A digital product is a file people pay to download: a Notion template (a pre-built page layout for Notion, a popular organizing app), a budgeting spreadsheet, a printable wedding planner, a pack of AI prompts (ready-made instructions you paste into ChatGPT), or design assets like fonts and icons. You make it once, upload it to a marketplace - an online store like Etsy or Gumroad that handles payment and delivery for you - and sell the same file over and over.

It is like baking one perfect cake, then selling unlimited photocopies of the recipe. The oven runs once; every later sale costs almost nothing - no shipping, no inventory. That is the appeal and the trap: anyone can photocopy a recipe, so everyone does, and the shelf is crowded.

## Why This Works in 2026

The demand is real. Notion, whose templates are the hottest category, hit roughly $600 million in revenue by the end of 2025 - a huge user base searching for templates someone else already built. Notion also opened its own Marketplace with built-in payments - a second storefront for creators. The digital planner market alone is projected to pass $7 billion by 2033.

But 2026 has a twist. AI tools flooded marketplaces with low-effort products, and buyers noticed. Research on Etsy trends shows shoppers now avoid listings that "look AI-generated." They pay extra for clear human quality: hand-drawn elements, a personal tone, hyper-specific solutions. Generic prompt packs stopped selling. Hyper-focused ones - prompts for one profession, one problem - still do. The junk flood helps careful sellers stand out - if you put in the craft.

## How the Money Actually Flows

1. You create a file - say, a $15 freelancer invoice tracker in Notion or Google Sheets.
2. You list it on a marketplace. On **Etsy**, you pay $0.20 to list. Per sale, Etsy takes a 6.5% transaction fee (its cut) plus 3% + $0.25 for payment processing (handling the card payment). On a $10 sale you keep about $8.75. On **Gumroad**, listing is free, but fees are now 10% + $0.50 per sale - cheapest to start, most expensive at scale. Alternatives like Payhip (5% on the free plan) or Lemon Squeezy (5% + $0.50) keep more in your pocket as volume grows.
3. A buyer finds your listing through marketplace search, Pinterest, TikTok, or your social posts. They pay and instantly download the file. The platform sends your share to your bank, usually within days.
4. You answer occasional support questions and update the product. That is the whole loop.

The real work hides in step 3. The file takes a weekend. Getting strangers to find it takes months.

## What You Need to Start

- **Skills:** Real fluency in one tool - Notion, Excel or Google Sheets, Canva, or an AI chatbot you understand deeply. Plus basic copywriting: writing product descriptions that explain the benefit, not just the features.
- **Tools:** Free tiers of Notion, Google Sheets, or Canva cover most products. Canva Pro (~$13/month) is optional for better mockup images - the preview pictures buyers see.
- **Money:** $0-100 total. Etsy listing fees are cents. Gumroad and Payhip are free until you sell. Some sellers spend $20-50 on a keyword-research tool (software showing what buyers type into search) or a few Etsy ads to test demand.
- **Time:** 5-10 hours per week for at least three months before judging results.

## Your First 90 Days

- **Weeks 1-2:** Pick one niche - a small, specific group of buyers - you actually understand. Think "budgeting templates for nurses," not "planners." Study the 10 best-selling competitors. Read their reviews, especially the complaints. Those are your product plan.
- **Weeks 3-4:** Build one genuinely good product that fixes those complaints. Make 5-8 clean mockup images and a short demo video.
- **Weeks 5-6:** List on Etsy (for search traffic) and on Gumroad or the Notion Marketplace (for direct links). Write keyword-rich titles based on what buyers actually type.
- **Weeks 7-10:** Market, don't build. Post the product in Reddit or Facebook communities where rules allow. Make 2-3 short TikTok or Pinterest posts per week showing the product in use. Offer a free mini-version to collect email addresses.
- **Weeks 11-13:** Read the data. Views but no sales? Fix the images and price. No views? The niche or keywords are wrong. Launch product #2 using what you learned. Sellers with 3-5 related products earn far more than one-product shops.

## Realistic Earnings Timeline

Be skeptical of income screenshots. Sober sources report:

- **Months 1-3:** $0-100 is the norm for a first template with minimal marketing. Many sellers earn nothing. First sales typically take 4-16 weeks. Sellers who launch deliberately - communities, demo videos, good SEO (search engine optimization: ranking well in search) - report $200-500 in a strong first month. They are the exception.
- **Months 4-6:** With 3-5 products and steady promotion, $100-500/month is realistic. Creator guides claim committed beginners can reach $500-1,500/month by month six - assuming near-perfect execution.
- **Year 1+:** Established sellers with a catalog, reviews, and an audience report $500-3,000/month. The strongest reach $3,000-8,000/month. The famous outliers - Thomas Frank ($1M in Notion template sales in 2022) and Easlo (over $500K total, around $20K/month) - built large audiences first. They are lottery tickets, not benchmarks.

## Will AI Kill This?

This is one of the more AI-exposed opportunities in this guide - hence 2/5. Anyone can now ask ChatGPT to "make me a budget spreadsheet," and Notion's own AI agents can assemble workspaces. Over 3-5 years, expect simple, generic products - basic planners, generic prompt lists - to become nearly worthless.

What survives: products where the value is curation and expertise, not the file itself. A tax-tracking spreadsheet from someone who understands freelancer taxes. A client-onboarding system from a real consultant. Templates bundled with tutorials and updates. Buyers in 2026 already pay extra for human-made, niche-specific quality because AI junk is everywhere. Build a small brand and an email list - buyer emails you own and can contact directly. That relationship is the part AI cannot replicate.

## Real-World Examples

- **Thomas Frank** (YouTuber): his team sold $1 million of Notion templates in calendar 2022, powered by a large existing YouTube audience, a full team, and years of content marketing.
- **Easlo (Jason Chin):** crossed $500,000 in total Notion template revenue, reportedly around $20K/month at peak, built through years of consistent Twitter posts and free templates that fed an email list.
- **The quiet majority:** community threads asking "how long until your first sale?" get answers from day one to *never*. One course creator reported four months to a first sale. Survivorship bias - you only hear from the winners - is severe here. The median seller earns very little.

## Common Mistakes Beginners Make

- Building for months before checking whether anyone searches for the product.
- Choosing saturated, generic niches ("daily planner") instead of specific ones.
- Ugly mockup images - on Etsy, the photo sells the file.
- Treating it as passive income and skipping marketing entirely.
- Uploading obvious AI-generated filler, which buyers now spot and avoid.
- Pricing at $2-3. Underpricing signals low quality and cannot fund ads.

## Pro Tips

- Read competitors' one-star reviews before building - a free product roadmap.
- List on two platforms: Etsy for search traffic, Gumroad or Payhip for direct links and higher margins.
- Give away a mini-version for an email address. Your list becomes your launch button for product #2.
- Watch fees as you scale. Above roughly $2,000/month, moving from Gumroad (10% + $0.50) to Payhip Pro or your own site raises take-home pay.
- Update products and message buyers about it. Updates trigger reviews, and reviews compound.
